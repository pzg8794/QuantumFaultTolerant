\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:RelatedWork}

Quantum networks distribute entanglement across repeaters and end-nodes to support long-distance quantum communication, distributed quantum computing, and sensing~\cite{wehner2018quantum,kimble2008quantum}. Unlike classical packet routing, quantum routing must operate with fragile states, probabilistic entanglement generation and swapping, decoherence, and fidelity loss~\cite{briegel1998quantum,dahlberg2021netsquid,bennett1993teleporting,zukowski1993event}. Across multi-hop paths, these effects make routing a repeated decision problem under uncertainty, where path choices must adapt to noisy outcomes and changing link conditions~\cite{li2025multipath,wang2025learning,huang2024quantum}.

A multi-armed bandit (MAB) models online routing as repeated action selection under partial feedback, where a learner selects candidate paths or allocation actions and updates from reward signals such as entanglement success or routing efficiency~\cite{lattimore2020bandit,bubeck2012regret}. We use this taxonomy to distinguish routing assumptions across model families: stochastic methods assume stable rewards~\cite{auer2002finite}, contextual and neural methods exploit predictive side information or nonlinear reward structure~\cite{chu2011contextual,zhou2020neuralucb}, adversarial methods address non-stationary or strategic rewards~\cite{auer2002nonstochastic}, and predictive methods incorporate forecasts~\cite{kar2024icmab}. These distinctions matter because benign noise, topology-dependent feedback, and adaptive disruption favor different forms of learning robustness~\cite{huang2024quantum}.

Quantum routing further couples path selection with resource allocation: the learner must decide both which route to use and how many qubits or attempts to allocate during each routing epoch. Replay semantics, bounded histories, and capped experience buffers can also affect stability under nonstationarity and vulnerability to adaptive attacks. We therefore evaluate routing robustness jointly across learning model, allocator strategy, replay-capacity configuration, and threat regime rather than treating these factors independently.

\subsection{Foundational, Contextual, and Hybrid Bandits}

Foundational bandit methods motivate the stochastic and adversarial baselines used in our evaluation. UCB-style optimism and Thompson-style posterior sampling provide canonical baselines under i.i.d. reward assumptions~\cite{auer2002finite,thompson1933likelihood}, while EXP3 provides an adversarial baseline without stochastic assumptions~\cite{auer2002nonstochastic}. Contextual bandits extend these methods by incorporating observable network state such as topology, load, or link quality. LinUCB models linear contextual rewards~\cite{li2010contextual}, while NeuralUCB and NeuralTS learn nonlinear reward representations~\cite{zhou2020neuralucb,zhang2022neuralts}.

Hybrid approaches combine robust exploration with structured exploitation, including pursuit-style learning automata~\cite{thathachar2011networks} and adversarial-neural hybrids such as \emph{EXPNeuralUCB}~\cite{huang2024quantum}, which integrates EXP3-style exploration with NeuralUCB-based reward modeling for joint path selection and qubit allocation. Predictive or informed bandits further incorporate forecasts or temporal dynamics using side information and time-series models such as ARIMA~\cite{kar2024icmab,box2015time}. Although predictive methods can proactively adapt to congestion or degradation, they may become fragile under delayed, biased, or adversarial forecasts.

\subsection{Quantum Network Routing with Online Learning}

Recent quantum-network work applies bandits and related online-learning methods to path selection under stochastic decoherence, online benchmarking signals, and structured disruption~\cite{wehner2018quantum,huang2024quantum,wang2025learning,li2025multipath,liu2024qbgp}. Wang \etal~\cite{wang2025learning} study learning high-quality paths under stochastic dynamics, Li \etal~\cite{li2025multipath} propose multipath inter-domain routing with online path selection, and Liu \etal~\cite{liu2024qbgp} use benchmarking signals to support adaptive routing. Wang \etal~\cite{wang2024adaptive} further formulate user-centric entanglement routing with long-term resource constraints and online routing/allocation decisions.

Huang \etal~\cite{huang2024quantum} propose \emph{EXPNeuralUCB}, a group neural bandit combining adversarial exploration with nonlinear reward modeling for joint path selection and qubit allocation. We use EXPNeuralUCB as one comparator within a broader robustness study that also evaluates pursuit--neural hybrids such as \texttt{CPursuitNeuralUCB} and \texttt{iCPursuitNeuralUCB}. This comparison separates changes in the learning rule from changes in allocator strategy and replay capacity.

Liu \etal~\cite{10621263} propose \emph{LinkSelFiE}, which addresses the link-level problem of selecting and estimating high-fidelity entanglement links when link qualities are unknown \emph{a priori}. They formulate link selection as a best-arm identification problem using benchmarking-driven estimation. In contrast, we target the end-to-end routing problem: joint path selection and qubit allocation under multiple threat regimes.

Beyond bandit-style routing, prior work studies learning-based route selection under noisy quantum-network conditions~\cite{chaudhary2023quantum}, RL-based adaptive routing with deep Q-networks~\cite{jallowkhan2025adaptive}, adaptive clustering through QuARC~\cite{clayton2024quarc}, hierarchical routing for scalability~\cite{cicconetti2024scalable}, repeater-aware routing~\cite{kumar2024routing}, and multipath cost-vector optimization~\cite{leone2021costvector}. These studies differ substantially in allocator assumptions, replay semantics, and threat models, making direct robustness comparisons difficult. Our benchmark addresses this comparability gap by evaluating multiple algorithm classes under the same threat, allocator, and replay-capacity grid.

\subsection{Benchmarking and Robustness Evaluation}

Quantum-network benchmarking frameworks motivate separating network behavior from policy behavior during evaluation~\cite{coopmans2021benchmark,kozlowski2022utility}, while best-of-both-worlds bandit theory motivates evaluation across stochastic and adversarial regimes~\cite{zimmert2019optimal}. Building on these ideas, we study routing robustness as an interaction among the learning model, allocator design, replay-capacity configuration, and threat regime rather than as a property of the routing policy alone.
