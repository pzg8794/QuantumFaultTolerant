\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}
% VALIDATED: All hard numbers corrected to match validated corpus tables (May 12 audit).
% Sources: tab:rq3a_master_informative, tab:rq2_adversarial, tab:rq3b_master_capacity_scaling, tab:rq3c_master_allocators.

This work evaluated quantum entanglement routing as a joint path-selection and qubit-allocation
problem under matched threat, allocator, and replay-capacity settings. Across the validated corpus,
the strongest results came from pursuit-based contextual and neural hybrids, especially
\texttt{CPursuitNeuralUCB} and \texttt{iCPursuitNeuralUCB}. These pursuit--neural hybrids
achieved 86--96\% Oracle-normalized efficiency with cross-scenario variance at or below 6.5\%,
and maintained robustness floors of 77--81\% across structured and adaptive threats, while
adversarial-first \texttt{EXPNeuralUCB} collapsed to a floor of 18\% under the same reactive
attacks.

The main result is that routing robustness is not determined by the learning rule alone.
Replay capacity, allocator choice, and threat regime interact: increasing replay scale can lift
Markov threat efficiency by up to 7.6~pp, while Adaptive-threat efficiency in the validated hybrid
corpus is invariant to replay scale (88.5\% across all $s$ values under the \texttt{Random}
allocator), confirming that capacity effects are threat-regime dependent rather than uniformly
beneficial. We refer to this threat-conditioned non-monotonicity as a capacity paradox.
Similarly, changing only the allocator can shift the worst-case floor for the same pursuit--neural
learner by up to 30~pp (\texttt{Fixed} floor~=~88.5\% vs.\ \texttt{Thompson} floor~=~58.4\%),
which means allocator policy is part of the robustness mechanism rather than a secondary
implementation detail.

These findings support a concrete deployment rule: a static default of
\texttt{iCPursuitNeuralUCB\,+\,Fixed\,+\,($T$-type, $s{=}2$)} sustains a 92.8\% worst-case
floor across all five threats at the 6K horizon; allocator and replay settings should then be
adjusted based on the detected threat regime rather than treated as a fixed resource knob.
Cross-testbed results confirm the same direction of effect, while also exposing scale limits
as larger or more constrained topologies narrow the gap to the Oracle. Future work should focus
on online threat classification, dynamic replay and capacity control, and hardware-grounded
validation on repeater testbeds.
