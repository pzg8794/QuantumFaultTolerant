\subsection{Grouped Synthesis Diagnostics}
\label{app:grouped_synthesis_diagnostics}

The grouped synthesis figure below remains in the appendix because it combines multiple diagnostic views without repeating the specific G9 panels now used in the main body. \Cref{fig:appendix_g8_advanced_synthesis} serves as the appendix-side reference point when readers want one grouped view of how the detailed diagnostics connect.

\begin{figure*}[t]
\centering
\textbf{Advanced Synthesis Diagnostics}\\[-0.0em]
\includegraphics[width=0.98\textwidth]{figures/icnp/ICNP-CODE-024_g8_advanced_4panel_grouped_full_figure.png}
\caption{Advanced synthesis diagnostic. Panel~(A) shows that the strongest contextual and pursuit--neural policies concentrate at lower Oracle gaps, supporting the claim that context-aware routing reduces efficiency loss relative to weaker baselines. Panel~(B) shows that replay scale changes performance non-monotonically: the intermediate setting produces the weakest robustness, while the larger setting recovers performance. This supports the capacity-paradox claim that capacity must be interpreted jointly with replay semantics and threat regime, not as a uniformly beneficial resource increase. Panel~(C) shows that allocator choice changes both average efficiency and tail behavior, supporting the claim that allocation policy is part of the robustness mechanism rather than a secondary implementation detail. Panel~(D) shows that the pursuit--neural advantage persists across external testbeds, while larger or more constrained topologies reduce separation from the Oracle and can change second-tier rankings. Together, the panels show that routing robustness is not determined by the learner alone, but by the interaction among policy family, replay/capacity setting, allocator choice, and testbed scale.}
